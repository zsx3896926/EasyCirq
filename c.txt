PCB: my precise fix made it worse - 2 violations became 3.

After moving R5 to an open area the board was at 2 violations, and neither involved R5:
PWR5V traces 1.4 and 4.1 mil from R4's ALARM pad against a 6 mil rule.

pcb.line.list filtered by geometry found the culprit - a segment along y=-1025 running
straight across R4's pad zone (R4 sits at y=-1027.7). Deleting it looked surgical.

  call pcb.route.delete ["f7ddbfc7429e2be7"]  -> ok, deleted
  pcb autoroute                               -> DRC 3: 1 clearance + 2 disconnected

Deleting the segment split PWR5V in two and the router did not reconnect it; a full
re-route does not either. The board went from one problem to two.

Two mistakes, both mine:

1. I took e289/e290 to be primitive ids. They are DRC error indices - that is why
   pcb.route.delete answered '2 id(s) not found'. The real id only came from
   pcb.line.list plus geometry.

2. I then tried to find leftover fragments with a union-find over coincident trace
   endpoints. It reported 34 'stray' segments, because much of PWR5V connects through
   pads rather than end-to-end. Following that list would have destroyed the supply net.
   The script was deleted without being run.

Lesson recorded: deleting a trace on a PCB breaks connectivity, so the check before
deleting is not 'is this in the way' but 'does this net still connect afterwards'. I only
checked the first.

Also confirmed along the way that DRC numbers were trustworthy - each count was taken
after save + doc reload, not from mutated in-memory state.

Schematic remains correct and verified. Package stays not orderable.

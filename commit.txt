PCB: 11 -> 6 -> 3 DRC violations, and the real gain came from undoing auto-place.

Full sequence, because the number alone does not say whether it was engineering or
shuffling:

  (236,-1408) outside the outline   -
  moved inside (320,-1085)          11
  after auto-place (667.8,-544.7)    6
  manual retry (540,-1095)           8
  scan of 6 candidates               5   best (300,-900)
  restore R1/R4 that auto-place had nudged   3
  90-degree corners                  3
  re-route PWR5V only                3
  scan 6 corridor candidates         8   best (450,-880)
  back to the best spot              3

The step that mattered was putting R1 (15 mil) and R4 (a few mil) back where they were.
auto-place had moved them to make room for R5, which invalidated a previously clean
routing. Restoring them took 5 violations down to 3. That was found by comparing against
the recorded coordinates, not by routing again.

Remaining three are all PWR5V traces crowding the R5_2 and R4_2 pads (0.9, 1.4, 4.1 mil
against a 6 mil rule). The router is deterministic here - repeated runs give the same
count, and neither corner style nor single-net re-routing changes it. This board is
19.49 x 21.72 mm with PWR5V already reaching six pins; after the pull-up it needs either
a hand-routed power channel, a copper pour, a topology change, or a few more mm.

Recorded four options with their costs rather than picking one - it is a design decision.

Schematic side is correct and verified (R4 = 1k, R5 = 10k pull-up, netlist checked).
Package stays not orderable: 3 DRC violations, Gerber not re-exported, gate not re-run.
